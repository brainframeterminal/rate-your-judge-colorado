These are counts of appealed decisions, not a score. A judge with more decisions appealed may simply hear more cases or cases more often appealed, and whether a ruling is changed depends partly on which appeals panel is drawn (see “Same law, different judge?”).

How to read this table

  • Only rulings that someone appealed appear here. Most rulings are never appealed, and there is no public count of every case a judge decided, so none of these numbers is a rate for a judge’s work as a whole.
  • “Changed on appeal” counts decisions that were reversed, affirmed in part and reversed in part, vacated or sent back (remanded). A ruling “affirmed and remanded with directions” counts as affirmed: the ruling stands and the trial court is told to take a further step. “Share of appealed” divides that count by the appealed decisions in the same row, and by nothing else.
  • A judge with a busy docket has more appeals, and more changed decisions, than a judge with a small one. Some types of cases are appealed far more often than others.
  • A remand can be narrow: for example a new calculation or one more finding, with the rest of the ruling intact.
  • A change in the law between the ruling and the appeal can cause a reversal that no judge could have avoided.
  • None of this is a finding of misconduct. Reviewing and correcting rulings is the normal work of an appeals court.

Where the numbers come from

  • The Court of Appeals’ own weekly announcements of its decisions, from June 2023, matched to the trial judge named in each one. How the matching works, and what each outcome and each published or unpublished opinion means, is on the Our method page.
  • Each judge’s profile also lists earlier decisions, from January 2013 to May 2023: unpublished decisions from the court’s own case-law database and published opinions from CourtListener (few for 2013–2014). Those years are not complete, so they are listed but not counted in this table, the comparison or any share.
  • Every judge’s own list, with a link to each decision, is on that judge’s profile. Updated every week.
  • How each number is calculated. Appealed decisions: every decision in those announcements on a ruling by a trial judge on this site, counted once. Changed on appeal: the decisions announced as reversed, affirmed in part and reversed in part, vacated or sent back. Share: changed ÷ appealed, rounded to a whole percent. Published or unpublished: as printed in the announcement.
