# Rating questions — what a layperson can actually see
Rule: ask about PROCESS the public can observe, one idea per question, never about legal correctness or the outcome.

## Judge (5 + overall stars; 1–5)
1. Did the judge let each side be heard? — Tyler's "voice"; NCSC CourTools M1 "chance to tell my side"; Arizona juror item 2.
2. Did the judge treat both sides the same? — "neutrality"; NCSC "treated the same as everyone else"; Arizona 6. Best layperson stand-in for impartiality.
3. Was the judge respectful to everyone? — "respect"; NCSC "courtesy and respect"; Arizona 1; ABA temperament.
4. Was it clear what the judge decided, and why? — NCSC "could follow / know what to do next"; Arizona 5; ABA communication.
5. Did the judge run things on time? — NCSC "reasonable amount of time" (lowest score in Rhode Island 2023); Arizona 7; OJPE "case management".
Deliberately left out: legal knowledge (laypeople cannot judge it; Utah/Arizona exclude it from the lay average), "prepared" (kept only for prosecutors).
## Prosecutor
Evidence is THIN: no official or validated prosecutor instrument exists; no live public prosecutor-rating site found. Items rest on ABA Prosecution Function Standards (4th ed., via snippets), Model Rule 3.8, Tyler's procedural-justice elements, the Stanford "Rate My District Attorney" blueprint (conviction rate is a bad measure; surveys of victims/witnesses/jurors/defendants), and victim/witness research (Fisher 2014; witness survey 2000; Milwaukee courtroom-communication experiment). Visible/invisible split is our inference.
1. Was the prosecutor respectful to everyone in the courtroom? (Tyler respect)
2. Did the prosecutor seem prepared? (preparation duty)
3. Did the prosecutor stick to the facts? Exaggerated ↔ Only facts (candor; against inflammatory argument, ABA 3-6.8 unverified)
4. Could you follow what the prosecutor was saying and doing? (Tyler understanding)
5. Did the prosecutor treat witnesses and the defendant fairly? (conduct, not motives — replaces "fair, not just out to win")
Plus on EVERY question a "I didn't see this" option (stored NULL, ≥3 of 5 must be answered) — defamation/accuracy safeguard.
Never ask: whether the verdict/sentence/plea was fair; "is the prosecutor good/honest/corrupt"; charging, disclosure (Brady), plea deals; politics; looks; "would you re-elect".
### BUILT 2026-10-12: victim/witness-only extras, shown to role=party who says they were a victim or witness
6. The prosecutor listened to me and kept me informed. (Fisher 2014, witness survey)  7. I felt safe and treated with dignity in the courtroom. (NDAA safety measure via Stanford)
Sub-role chips (defendant/family · victim/family · witness · someone else) at the top of the rate form for role=party; the two extras appear only for prosecutors + victim/witness, below the five main questions; stored in x1/x2 and shown in a SEPARATE card on the profile (n ≥ 2), never merged into the main average.
## Outcome bias
Optional chips "I won / I lost / Mixed / I only watched"; the profile shows "even among people who said they lost: X" when n ≥ 2 (Dutch litigant study: process predicts trust at both outcomes).
